Physics-Attested Federated Learning — Pilot Evaluation Against Fixed Criteria

Outcomes of the five success criteria fixed in scripts/go_nogo.py, together with the threat model, before any experiment was run, evaluated on the actual pilot result files.

Four of the five criteria met their thresholds. Criterion 4b — that projection onto the physics manifold must not increase damage on any baseline defence — was not met for FLTrust. Because the decision script combines all criteria with a strict logical AND, its literal output recommends against proceeding. On inspection, the 4b result is an unanticipated interaction effect between physics projection and similarity-weighted aggregation — a finding that motivates the full study rather than an argument for terminating it. Recommendation: proceed to the full study, with the FLTrust interaction stated in advance as a named research question (RQ3).

Criterion 1 · Residual separation

Physics invariants separate three of four fabrications

Do the physics residuals of honest and fabricated data form distinguishable populations?
Fraction of rows violating the invariants on real BATADAL telemetry, against the 1% batch-admission threshold — for the hand-crafted expert set (9 invariants) and the automatically mined set (10 invariants). Honest batches: 0.000 false rejection under both.

Hand-crafted (expert, 9) Mined (automatic, 10)

Disclosed evasion: regime splicing breaks physics only at splice points (0.16% of rows), which sits below the 1% threshold, so spliced batches are admitted. This is a structural property of percentage-based thresholds; sequence-based run checks are proposed for the full study. Miner validation: the automatically mined set matches or exceeds the expert set on every fabrication — it discovers one true coupling (valve V2) the hand-written set omits — so automated discovery loses nothing against expert knowledge, which licenses the method for plants with no documented invariants.

Criteria 2 & 3 · The vulnerability

Defences that accept fabricated updates lose detection accuracy

Simulated plant, 10 clients, 25 rounds, two seeds. Change in F1 (percentage points) when 3 of 10 clients train on fabricated data, per aggregation rule. The chip under each bar is the fraction of malicious updates that rule accepted.

Three of six rules (FedAvg, coordinate-wise median, norm clipping) accepted 100% of fabricated updates — meeting the threshold of at least three set in the evaluation protocol. Damage tracks acceptance: FedAvg loses 34.6 points and trimmed mean 15.2, while Krum, which rejects the updates, is nearly unaffected. Median accepts the updates yet improves slightly (+3.7), a reminder that acceptance and damage are measured independently.

What Criterion 3 measures, and why it is separate from Criterion 2. The acceptance chip (Criterion 2) measures the door: how much of the malicious clients' contribution entered the global model. The bar (Criterion 3) measures the damage: how far the shared detector's F1 fell once that contribution was inside. A vulnerability claim requires both — acceptance without damage is harmless (median admits everything and loses nothing), and filtering without safety is an illusion (trimmed mean blocks two-thirds of the poison and the remaining third still costs 15.2 points). The threshold in the evaluation protocol — at least one defence losing ≥ 3.0 F1 points — asks only that some widely-deployed rule sits at the dangerous conjunction of open door and real harm. FedAvg, the industry default, sits squarely on it: 100% acceptance, −34.6 points, a detector reduced from working (F1 ≈ 0.53) to decorative (F1 ≈ 0.18).
Criterion 4 · The physics gate

Requiring physical consistency removes the poisoning attack — except against FLTrust

Simulated plant. Mean F1 of the shared detector on the simulated plant's held-out test attacks, across seeds 0–2, per aggregation rule, in three federations that differ only in what 3 of the 10 clients train on: honest — all 10/10 clients train on their own honest telemetry; poisoning attack — 3/10 clients train on channel-roll fabrications that violate the physics invariants, with no physics check in place; poisoning + physics — the same 3/10 clients, but the check is deployed and the adaptive attacker projects each fabricated batch onto the physics manifold so it passes. The drop from the first bar to the second is the harm the poisoning attack does when nothing stops it. The third bar shows the detector once the physics check is deployed and the attacker has adapted to it: if it returns to the height of the first bar, the requirement removed the attack's effect entirely (FedAvg: 0.55 → 0.22 → 0.56, the +0.01 is seed noise); if it stays below the first bar, some harm still gets through (FLTrust: 0.53 → 0.51 → 0.47, where it gets worse).

Honest (10/10 clients, no attack) Poisoning attack (3/10 clients, channel roll, non-physics-invariant) Poisoning + physics (3/10 clients, channel roll projected to pass the check)

Under FedAvg, which accepts every update, the poisoning attack drops the detector from F1 0.55 to 0.22; with the physics requirement in place the same attacker lands at 0.56, indistinguishable from the honest federation. Against FedAvg and trimmed mean the physics requirement removes 104.2% and 110.5% of the attack's F1 damage — above 100% because the poisoning + physics runs sit slightly above honest, which is noise. Against FLTrust the sign reverses: mean damage grows from 2.3 to 6.3 points once the attacker satisfies the physics constraints (see 4b). The adaptive attacker here knows the invariant set exactly but does not optimise against the aggregation rule; it submits the nearest physics-feasible batch to its original fabrication.

Criterion 4b · The interaction effect

Why projection helps the attacker against FLTrust

FLTrust weights clients by cosine similarity to a clean server reference. Projecting a fabricated batch onto plant physics rotates its gradient toward the honest direction, so FLTrust's acceptance of the malicious client rises sharply — consistently in every seed.

Fabricated Projected

The acceptance jump is stable across seeds, but the resulting extra damage varies widely — the projected attack costs 13.5, 4.9, and 0.7 F1 points in seeds 0, 1, and 2. The mechanism is reliable; its downstream effect size is not yet, which is exactly why the full study states this interaction in advance as a named research question across five seeds and four malicious fractions.

Dataset scope

Where the method applies, and where it does not

BATADAL — primary benchmark in scope

C-Town municipal water network. The miner recovers 10 invariants (5 pump–flow couplings, 4 tank mass balances, 1 valve coupling); honest false rejection is 0.000 and three of four fabrication recipes are fully separated. Hand-crafted and mined invariant sets agree (96.7% vs 98.3% violation rates on channel roll).

HAI — stated scope boundary out of scope

Boiler–turbine testbed, 80+ channels. Only 3 invariants are minable because the training data contains near-zero actuator switching across 920k+ rows — a structural property of the dataset, verified directly, not a defect of the miner. Reported as the method's scope boundary: physics attestation requires observable actuation dynamics.
